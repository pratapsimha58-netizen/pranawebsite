# Idea shortlist: six Western models an Indian consumer already pays for

## Screening criteria

Ideas were screened against the brief: a replica of a Western business, financially sustainable, paid for by Indian consumers, startable by a solo founder with under INR 1 lakh. Ideas that need inventory, a kitchen, a licence, a hardware component or a two-sided marketplace at launch were removed before scoring.

Each surviving idea is scored 1-5 on six criteria. Weights reflect the brief: proof of willingness-to-pay and capital fit matter most.

| Criterion | Weight | What a 5 looks like |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Evidence Indians already pay | 25% | Multiple Indian companies sell the same thing at the same price point today |
| Capital fit (under INR 1L) | 20% | Year-one fixed costs under INR 50,000 with no working-capital cycle |
| Solo feasibility | 20% | One person can sell, deliver and support the first 20 customers |
| Gross margin | 15% | Above 70% at launch |
| Competition and defensibility | 10% | Fragmented, low-quality supply; room to win on trust and outcomes |
| Regulatory and liability risk | 10% | No licence, no physical safety exposure, no regulated advice |

## The six models

### 1. TopResume / Teal: productised career services

Western model: TopResume sells done-for-you resume, cover letter and LinkedIn rewrites at USD 149-449 per package. Teal sells job-search software at USD 9-29 per month. Both grew on the back of ATS anxiety among job seekers.

Indian evidence: Naukri FastForward (resume writing at INR 1,800-6,000), Preplaced and Topmate (mentor and mock-interview sessions at INR 1,000-5,000), plus thousands of freelancers on LinkedIn and Fiverr. Job switching is frequent (Indian IT attrition has run at 15-25% a year) and the notice-period culture means switchers plan months ahead and are willing to invest.

India adaptation: INR pricing at one-third of the Western equivalent, WhatsApp delivery, Naukri-friendly resume formats, CTC and notice-period negotiation scripts, and interview prep aimed at Indian service companies and product startups.

### 2. Papa / Honor: NRI-funded parent care

Western model: Papa matches older adults with "Papa Pals" for companionship and errands, paid by health plans; Honor sells home-care hours.

Indian evidence: Emoha, Samarth Eldercare and Anvayaa sell monthly parent-care memberships to NRI children at INR 3,000-15,000 a month. Strong willingness-to-pay in USD.

Why it lost: high operational liability (a stranger in an elderly parent's home), needs background-checked staff from day one, a single incident can end the business, and delivery is bound to one city. Capital fit is fine, but solo feasibility and risk scores are poor.

### 3. Noom: cohort-based habit and nutrition coaching

Western model: Noom sells app-plus-coach weight-loss programmes at USD 60-70 a month.

Indian evidence: HealthifyMe, Fittr and Cult.fit sell coach-led plans at INR 1,500-5,000 a month. Demand is real.

Why it lost: the market is already dominated by well-funded incumbents with apps and thousands of coaches; a solo founder competes on price against them. Health claims carry regulatory and reputational risk. Retention past 8 weeks is notoriously poor, which undermines the "sustainable" requirement.

### 4. YNAB / Maven: personal-finance cohort course for first-jobbers

Western model: YNAB sells budgeting software at USD 109 a year; Maven hosts paid cohort courses at USD 300-2,000.

Indian evidence: Zerodha Varsity (free), finance creators selling courses at INR 2,000-10,000, and a large first-jobber population with rising salaries.

Why it lost: anything that edges toward "which fund should I buy" needs SEBI registration as an investment adviser or research analyst. Staying on the safe side of that line limits the product to budgeting, which is the part Indians are least willing to pay for. Regulatory score drags it down.

### 5. Zola: wedding planning and vendor shortlisting

Western model: Zola combines a wedding registry, planning tools and vendor marketplace, monetised through registry commissions and vendor listings.

Indian evidence: WedMeGood and WeddingWire India sell vendor listings; Indian families spend INR 10-50 lakh on weddings and hire planners at 5-10% of budget.

Why it lost: the consumer part (planning tools) is free in India; the money is in vendor commissions, which requires a two-sided marketplace. Seasonal, high-touch, and the buyer is often the parents rather than the couple. Solo feasibility and capital fit are weak once you need vendor supply.

### 6. Havenly: async online interior design

Western model: Havenly sells online interior design packages at USD 129-249 per room.

Indian evidence: Livspace and HomeLane sell full-service interiors at INR 3-15 lakh; Pinterest-driven demand among renters and first-home buyers is visible, but paid online-only consults are rare.

Why it lost: Indians pay for execution (furniture, carpentry, installation), not for design advice alone. Converting an online consult into revenue means either taking a cut of execution (needs vendor supply) or convincing customers to pay for a PDF, which has not been proven at scale in India.

## Why career services wins under INR 1 lakh

1. **Paid demand is already proven at the intended price.** Naukri, the largest job portal in India, sells the same service at INR 1,800-6,000. The founder does not need to educate the market; the job is to be visibly better than Naukri's templated output and the freelancer lottery.
2. **Capital goes to acquisition, not operations.** Fixed costs are a domain, a Google Workspace seat, an ATS-scanning tool, a Razorpay account and registration. Roughly half the INR 1 lakh is left for testing ads and content, which is where a solo founder learns fastest.
3. **Delivery scales in steps.** The founder writes the first 30-50 resumes, learns what converts, builds a rubric, then contracts writers at INR 700-1,000 per resume. Each step is funded by revenue from the previous one.
4. **Repeat revenue exists.** The same customer switches jobs every 2-3 years and refers colleagues during every round of layoffs or appraisal season. Interview prep and the sprint tier raise average order value without new customers.
5. **Low downside.** Worst case is a refund, not a lawsuit or an injured parent.

The main weakness is commoditisation: cheap resume writers and free AI tools exist. The plan addresses this in [06-operations-and-risks.md](06-operations-and-risks.md) by positioning on outcomes (interview call-backs), turnaround guarantees and a human review call, none of which a Fiverr seller or a chatbot offers reliably.

## Runner-up

Parent care for NRI families remains the strongest alternative if the founder later has capital for staff, insurance and background checks. It has the highest revenue per customer and USD billing, but is not a solo, sub-INR-1L business.
